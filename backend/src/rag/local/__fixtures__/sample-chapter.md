If you had to choose, would you spend your pocket money on snacks or save it
for a new pair of shoes? These are not random questions; rather, these are
examples of economic choices that individuals, enterprises, and governments
have to make.

Some of our preferences are *needs*, such as essentials like food, water, and
shelter, while others are *wants*, like gadgets, vacations, or luxury items.

**Choices and Limited Resources**

[GLOSSARY TERM | Page 184]
Term: Resources
Definition: Factors used for the production of goods and services. These can
be natural, like water and coal, or human-made, like capital and technology.
[/GLOSSARY TERM]

Resources are required to satisfy human needs and wants. However, both natural
and human-made resources are limited in quantity. When one alternative is
chosen, the other options are given up. The value of what is given up is known
as the **opportunity cost**.

[GLOSSARY TERM | Page 185]
Term: Production Possibility Curve (PPC)
Definition: The curve showing different combinations of goods that can be
produced using all available resources.
[/GLOSSARY TERM]

This is called the **Production Possibility Curve (PPC)**, which shows the
trade-off between barley and wheat produced. To grow more barley, some wheat
must be sacrificed - this is the opportunity cost of growing barley.
